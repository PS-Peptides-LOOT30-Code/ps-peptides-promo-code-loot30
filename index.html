HPLC and mass spectrometry: why PSPeptides lists both

High-performance liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry answer different analytical questions. HPLC separates components in a sample according to their behavior in a chromatographic system. Under an appropriate method, the resulting signals can be used to characterize relative purity and reveal detectable related species.

Mass spectrometry analyzes ions by mass-to-charge ratio. In peptide characterization, mass information can help determine whether the observed molecule is consistent with the expected molecular identity. Used together, chromatography and mass spectrometry can therefore provide stronger analytical information than either phrase used as a standalone marketing badge.

The PSPeptides certificates examined for this resource describe “HPLC With UV Detection Coupled With Mass Spectrometry.” They also show separate fields for mass identification, HPLC purity, and mass content. That separation is valuable because the three fields should not be merged into the vague statement “the peptide tested at 99%.”

An analytical report is most useful when the laboratory can answer: What was tested? When? Under what identifier? By which laboratory? What analytical method is stated? What identity was reported? What was the HPLC result? What was the measured content?

Third-party testing is valuable—but “independent” and “accredited” are not identical claims

PSPeptides describes its certificates as coming from independent, accredited laboratories. The public certificates examined here identify North American Diagnostics rather than PSPeptides as the issuing laboratory. That supports the narrower observation that the displayed report is issued under a separate laboratory identity.

But third-party status and accreditation answer different questions. Independence concerns the organizational relationship between seller and tester. Accreditation refers to formal recognition of laboratory competence under a defined program and scope.

ISO/IEC 17025 is the principal international standard for testing and calibration laboratories. ISO describes it as setting requirements for competence, impartiality, and consistent laboratory operation. Accreditation should also be evaluated by scope: a laboratory can hold accreditation for certain activities without every method necessarily falling within that scope.

The one-page PSPeptides COAs reviewed for this article do not themselves display an ISO/IEC 17025 certificate number, accrediting body, or accreditation scope. That observation is not equivalent to claiming that no accreditation exists. It simply means the summary certificate itself does not provide enough information to independently confirm that particular claim.

Purity, heavy metals, endotoxin, sterility, and other screens are separate quality dimensions

One of the better features of PSPeptides’ Certifications interface is that it does not label every test simply as “purity.” For many products, separate options are presented for the COA, heavy metals, endotoxin, sterility, and fentanyl screening.

Those categories should remain separate in a research file. HPLC purity primarily addresses chromatographic composition under a defined method. Heavy-metal analysis concerns specified elemental contaminants. Endotoxin testing evaluates bacterial endotoxins under the relevant assay. Sterility testing addresses viable microbial contamination. A fentanyl screen concerns the specified target and the detection capability of the method.

Passing one does not automatically imply passing another. A high chromatographic purity result does not mathematically prove sterility. A favorable sterility result does not establish molecular identity. An identity result does not automatically establish nominal content. A heavy-metals report does not answer every question about organic impurities.

The buyer should therefore decide which quality attributes are material to the planned in-vitro work and inspect the appropriate reports rather than treating several “Lab Tested” links as one universal certificate.
